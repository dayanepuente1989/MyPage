01 About

Ornithologist, bioacoustician, night listener

I’m a PhD candidate in Conservation and Sustainable Use of Natural Resources at the Instituto Politécnico Nacional (CIIDIR Unidad Oaxaca), where I work in the Laboratory of Bioacoustics and Behavioral Ecology. My research brings together ornithology, bioacoustics and wildlife management: I use bird vocalizations to understand how nocturnal birds behave across mountain landscapes, and I study the relationship between wild birds and the farmers whose crops they feed on.

My path began in agroecology, continued through wildlife management on the coast of Oaxaca, and now centres on nightjars, a group of cryptic nocturnal birds known mostly by their voices. Along the way I have coordinated urban birdwatching workshops, brought birds and bioacoustics into schools in the Sierra de Oaxaca, and worked in sea turtle and bird banding camps.

Doctoral research · IPN CIIDIR Oaxaca

Voices of the night: nightjars in Oaxaca’s highlands

Nightjars (Caprimulgidae) are hard to see but easy to hear. My doctoral research follows the vocal activity of two montane Neotropical nightjars along an altitudinal gradient in the mountains of Oaxaca, asking how elevation, moonlight and the availability of insect prey shape when and how much they sing. It builds on earlier work on the geographic variation and behavioural ecology of the Mexican Whip-poor-will (Antrostomus arizonae).

  • How does vocal activity change with elevation?
  • How does moonlight shape nightly singing?
  • Does singing track the availability of prey?

Human–wildlife interactions · Oaxaca

Birds, maize and farmers

Some wild birds feed on maize, a staple crop across rural Oaxaca. I evaluated birds feeding on maize in Santa María Colotepec and the Sierra Madre del Sur, proposed management alternatives, and documented how farmers in the Costa region perceive these birds, a step towards coexistence that works for both people and wildlife.

  • Social-ecological systems
  • ATLAS.ti
  • Crop damage assessment

Early research · UASLP, San Luis Potosí

Roots in agroecology

My first projects combined farming and wildlife: the breeding ecology of the Barn Swallow (Hirundo rustica) at UASLP’s Faculty of Agronomy and Veterinary Medicine, which won second place in the poster presentations at UASLP’s Week of Science in 2018, and the development of two bean varieties under heat stress.

  • Breeding ecology
  • Agroecology

Environmental education

Birds for everyone

I bring birds and bioacoustics into schools in the Sierra de Oaxaca, help create bird- and pollinator-friendly gardens, and have coordinated the Urban Bird Program (PAU) Ojo de Lumbre with CONABIO in San Luis Potosí, running workshops and talks on birdwatching and why birds matter.
